## Review question and objectives (CoCoPop)

> Condition: avian influenza virus infection/detection, including HPAI and LPAI with subtype, clade/lineage, reassortment, mutation and diversity evidence. Context: Nigeria, 1 January 2006–20 September 2026. Population: domestic poultry (commercial, backyard, live-bird markets), wild/migratory/wetland birds and Nigeria-linked isolates. Central question: How have avian influenza viruses emerged, evolved genetically, and redistributed spatially and temporally in Nigeria from 2006 to 2026? Objectives: (1) temporal/first-detection; (2) geographic by state/zone/epoch; (3) host range/system shifts; (4) clade replacement, diversification, reassortment/mutation with evidence grading.

## Selection process — AI-assisted screening and human verification (PRISMA 2020 Item 8; draft 2026-09-29)

> Supersedes the screening sentence in the stub above, which does not describe the AI-assisted process actually used.

Records were screened in two stages (title/metadata, then full text) by two large language model agents working independently and blind to each other's decisions, using the piloted screening form (Supplementary Appendix). Reviewer 1 was an executor agent and Reviewer 2 a separate reviewer agent (OpenAI Codex agents in the first screening rounds [confirm the exact agent per round against the file 15 amendment log before submission]; thereafter Anthropic Claude Opus 5.5 as Reviewer 1 and Claude Sonnet 5, later Sonnet 5.5, as Reviewer 2; changes are dated in the protocol amendment log). Disagreements were adjudicated by a third AI agent (the coordinating session) against the protocol criteria; at the title stage a record retained by either reviewer proceeded to full text. Each full-text exclusion was assigned one reason from a fixed hierarchy, with page-level evidence and the source of the full text recorded. Inter-reviewer agreement was 97.0% at the title/metadata stage (κ = 0.88; 1,877 records), and 86.6% at full text (κ = 0.76; 216 reports), or 95.3% (κ = 0.90) where both reviewers gave a definitive decision; for the Google Scholar update search it was 94.3% (κ = 0.77; 228 records) at the title stage and 19/20 reports at full text.

AI screening decisions were then verified by the author (M.O.). The verification set comprised 429 decisions: all full-text inclusions and exclusions, all duplicate-record removals, a seeded random 10% sample of title-stage exclusions, and all title-stage adjudication overrides. Of these, 107 were designated priority decisions (reviewer disagreements, adjudication overrides, borderline cases, duplicate removals and the most recent screening batches). For each priority decision, the author reviewed a summary of the record, the AI decision and the extracted evidence, and then gave a decision. For accessibility reasons, these summaries were prepared by an AI assistant, and the author's decisions were given in writing and transcribed by the assistant. The author also set three eligibility rules to resolve inconsistencies identified during verification (secondary analyses, official outbreak records, reprinted tables). The author changed six of the 107 priority decisions: four full-text exclusions became inclusions, and two title-stage exclusions were sent to full-text assessment. The remaining 322 lower-priority decisions were confirmed in a single statement rather than individually. During report-to-study linkage, a further report included by the AI reviewers was found to be a multi-country sequence compilation whose only Nigerian datum was already reported in included studies; the author excluded it. The author later re-checked all 107 priority decisions a second time, entering each answer directly in a one-row-per-screen tool that showed the current decision and the reviewers' evidence; all 107 were reconfirmed without change. The verification sheet is available as supplementary material.

**Limitations text (for the Discussion):** screening was conducted by AI agents with author confirmation rather than by two independent human reviewers; the human check covered 107 priority decisions individually, but it relied on AI-prepared summaries rather than an independent re-reading of each full text, and the remaining 322 verified decisions were confirmed in a single statement; and most title-stage exclusions were made on title and metadata alone, because many database records lacked abstracts. These factors increase the risk that relevant reports were missed or misclassified.
